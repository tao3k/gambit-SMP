** Retired authority
The former ticket wait read volatile =current_ticket= without an acquire.
The collective had separate =sync_id0/1/2= and =sync_op0/1/2= fields;
=SYNC_GET_MSG= reset its flag before copying the payload. A full fence after
writing a flag does not by itself give a volatile reader an acquire edge.
The replacement uses one ordered word service and three fixed SPSC inboxes.
It removes the split flags/payload macros and =___sync_wait= entry. It does
not retire the unrelated legacy CAS APIs or the physical member barrier.

* Conditional memory-order proof
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ordered-sync-proof
:PRECONDITIONS: FIXED-MEMBERSHIP; SINGLE-PRODUCER-CONSUMER-PER-EDGE; ALIGNED-WORDS; PHYSICAL-WRITER-EXCLUSION
:END:
1. Ticket reservation uses relaxed fetch-add: it allocates an order, not entry
   permission. The acquire load admitting ticket k reads the release increment
   from its predecessor; ordinary protected writes happen before the new owner.
   Trylock first acquires current, then conditionally reserves next. Outstanding
   tickets must remain below the modulo ticket namespace.
2. The producer writes the entire slot payload before release-publishing READY.
   The consumer acquires READY, copies all fields, then release-publishes EMPTY.
   The next producer acquires EMPTY before overwriting. No payload access races
   with reuse under this SPSC lifetime. Repeating stamp values is conditional
   on single-producer/consumer coherence and exclusive initialization, not an
   admission of arbitrary late readers or migrated workers.
3. Recipient mutex serializes wait registration and predicate checks. Three
   inbox predicates share one CV, so both publish and ACK broadcast. A signal
   could wake a waiter for the wrong predicate and strand the enabled waiter.
   Spurious wakeups always recheck. Backpressure keeps the local operation
   owned by the blocked producer. The existing 2000-spin threshold is unchanged.
4. Upward acquire/release edges propagate every fixed member's frozen roots;
   downward edges publish the selected operation. Final tree completion joins
   repairs before resume. This is visibility plus actual arrivals, not a claim
   that a fence stops a running thread. A slot cannot be reinitialized merely
   because a join or owner-stop result is unknown.

GCC/Clang =__atomic= builtins on naturally aligned Scheme/native word storage
are a compiler-extension refinement; these objects are not declared ISO
=_Atomic=. Native acquire/release orders and the conservative full-RMW backend
share the API. Only exclusive construction may initialize stamp storage with
a plain write: a fallback CAS must not read an indeterminate initial value.
See [[https://gcc.gnu.org/onlinedocs/gcc/_005f_005fatomic-Builtins.html][GCC atomic builtin semantics]] and [[https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg14/www/docs/n3220.pdf][C working draft N3220]] for synchronization
and atomic-coherence premises. Native tests cannot prove every weak-memory
execution. Generated assembly / LLVM IR confirms emitted orders, not fairness.

* Counterexamples and failure custody
** Early EMPTY
If a receiver publishes EMPTY before copying args, the sender can overwrite
args while the receiver reads. READY visibility alone does not protect reuse.
The replacement copies under the recipient mutex, then ACKs; kernel evidence
also exercises the actual slot macros without that additional mutex.

** Initialization and supersession
The first candidate used a CAS-backed store to initialize unknown storage.
Static review rejected it. The owned build was stopped before native admission;
its terminal receipt and missing child receipt remain historical evidence.
The corrected initializer is an exclusive pre-publication assignment. No
successful step is inferred from the interrupted attempt.

The framework originally passed that nonexistent child path to its resume
reader. The transport repair treats a launched, reaped, quiescent attempt as
accounting only. It still rejects unchanged failures, live groups and expanded
budgets. Its Python regression tests belong to the existing SMP framework;
Gambit fixtures and orchestration remain C / native Scheme.

** Unknown owner
Unknown join, in-flight native writes, member-slot reuse and debug-history
ownership remain open. Acquiring a stamp cannot establish writer death or
physical exclusion. Preserve source roots and member storage until the
existing owner/stop obligations authorize reclamation. No current result
closes full carrier retirement or independent-worker migration.

** Per-message ordering budget
The former send macro issued one standalone full fence after setting the flag.
That explicit fence is removed. Each new message has a READY release and an
EMPTY release, acquire observations, plus the existing sender mutex and the
new receive-copy mutex pair. Thus a P-member tree replaces =2(P-1)= explicit
full fences with =4(P-1)= release stores and its acquire observations, and adds
=2(P-1)= receive mutex pairs and ACK broadcasts. Publication broadcasts replace
the former signals. Fallback RMW implementations can be stronger and more
expensive than those abstract release stores. This operation inventory is a
mechanism comparison, not a prediction that the new synchronization is faster.

** Emitted acquire instruction
The first local checker expected LDAR specifically and rejected the actual
GCC output. That checker rejection is retained. The compiler selected
=armv8.5-a= and emitted =LDAPR=, =STLR=, =LDADD= and =LDADDL=; Clang IR contains
acquire loads, release stores/RMW and monotonic reservation RMW. The corrected
checker accepts the documented acquire mapping, not an arbitrary load opcode.
[[https://gcc.gnu.org/onlinedocs/gcc/AArch64-Options.html][GCC's AArch64 RCpc documentation]] specifies LDAPR for acquire atomic loads;
[[https://support.arm.com/documentation/dui0801/l/A64-Data-Transfer-Instructions/LDAPR--A64-][Arm's LDAPR definition]] also explains its weaker store-release/load-acquire
ordering. The protocol relies on C acquire/release synchronization, not an
unrequested sequentially consistent store-to-load fence. This qualification
is compiler/target-specific and does not certify every machine execution.
