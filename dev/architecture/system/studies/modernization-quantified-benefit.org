workload or model checker is run. Arithmetic tables are parameter substitutions,
not fitted estimates of Gambit. Read the [[file:../../runtime/gc/studies/gc-current-mechanism-audit.org][current GC audit]]
and [[file:../../runtime/gc/studies/gc-native-runtime-design.org][full candidate]].

**Decision: investigate the modernization of Gambit's compiler/runtime contract
and collector as the primary technical hypothesis. Keep direct Zig realization
as a competing full implementation. Do not make the modernization depend on
proving that Zig must win. The present evidence does not justify committing to
a full Zig rewrite on economic grounds. It does justify a precise conditional
work-reduction theorem and concrete language-selection thresholds.**

*** A Source-Grounded Work-Reduction Theorem
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 4115da6b-11bc-53b4-9b19-15e8d6aa2b3b
:CUSTOM_ID: a-source-grounded-work-reduction-theorem
:END:

QB01. Fix a finite sequence of K successful collection cuts, and the same
logical allocation/mutation/root history at those cuts in both designs.
This is a counterfactual accounting alignment, not a claim that differently
sized heaps naturally collect equally often. Consider ordinary, initially young,
movable heap objects handled by the current mark_array copy loop [Q-S01].
Exclude still/permanent objects, specialized continuation-frame copying,
headers/padding, failed attempts and objects already mature before the horizon.
For object o, let s(o) be its fixed payload bytes and k(o) the number of these
cuts at which it remains live, with k(o)>=1. Variable-sized/shrunk payloads need
a per-cut size sum rather than the constant-size formula below.

The target has direct survivor promotion, no mature movement/compaction in this
scope, unique complete forwarding and complete roots/barriers. Failure of these
semantic premises invalidates the target; it cannot be priced as an optimization.
The small movable allocation route is real [Q-S02]; large native still allocations
are outside this theorem. No application lifetime distribution is assumed known.

QB02. In the current source, each successful full collection copies the live
object's payload through mark_array. The header is preserved, so that object's
survival does not exempt it from copying at the next cut [Q-S01]. By induction
over its live cuts, its payload-copy work is s(o)*k(o). The target copies it once
on its first minor survival and leaves the mature body nonmoving thereafter.
Summing object contributions gives:

#+BEGIN_SRC text :eval never
current_payload_copy = sum_o s(o) * k(o)
target_payload_copy  = sum_o s(o)
avoided_payload_copy = sum_o s(o) * (k(o) - 1)
weighted_survival    = sum_o s(o) * k(o) / sum_o s(o)
fraction_avoided     = 1 - 1 / weighted_survival
#+END_SRC

This is an exact conditional result for the specified logical payload-copy
component. It is not a proof about total collector work, machine memory traffic,
pause time, application speed or engineering ROI. Dead-before-first-cut objects
contribute zero to both sums. New major tracing and barriers still cost work.

QB03. The following rows are exact arithmetic, not workload predictions:

| Payload-weighted live cuts | Current copies per payload byte | Target copies | This component avoided |
|-
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 2 | 1 | 50% |
| 5 | 5 | 1 | 80% |
| 10 | 10 | 1 | 90% |
| 100 | 100 | 1 | 99% |

For a hypothetical fixed 64 MiB cohort live at all 100 cuts, current payload
copying is 6400 MiB = 6.25 GiB; the target copies 64 MiB once. The difference is
6336 MiB = 6.1875 GiB. Logical payload loads plus stores avoid twice that operand
volume, *not necessarily twice that DRAM traffic*: caches, code generation,
headers, metadata and other collector traffic are separate. No 64 MiB cohort or
100-cut behavior has been observed in this project.

*** From Work Reduction to Program Benefit
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: a109d9e1-e740-5e74-8e51-441718573c75
:CUSTOM_ID: from-work-reduction-to-program-benefit
:END:

QB04. Fix a valid additive, nonoverlapping baseline time/cost partition T.
Let f be the fraction attributable to the cohort's old copying, q its target
copying cost divided by its old copying cost, and a all additional net costs
outside that component divided by T. Changes in major tracing, stores, roots,
coordination, fragmentation-induced collection and allocation belong in a;
include any independent savings there with their sign. Then:

#+BEGIN_SRC text :eval never
target / baseline = 1 - f + q*f + a
time_saved_ratio  = f*(1-q) - a
speed_ratio      = 1 / (1 - f + q*f + a)
positive_time_condition: a < f*(1-q)
#+END_SRC

A byte reduction supplies q only under an additional cost-per-byte assumption.
For equal effective copy cost per payload byte and weighted_survival=100,
q=0.01. The following sensitivity table assumes that equality and a=0.05.
It is *not* a forecast of Gambit, Zig or any actual workload:

| Hypothetical baseline cohort-copy fraction f | Additional cost a | Total time saved | Baseline time / target time |
|-
| 5% | 5% | -0.05% (regression) | 0.9995x |
| 20% | 5% | 14.8% | 1.1737x |
| 50% | 5% | 44.5% | 1.8018x |

Even eliminating this component completely cannot save more than f of baseline
time when all other costs stay equal. Parallel overlap makes a naive CPU-time
sum unsuitable for wall-time prediction; a valid elapsed-time partition or an
explicit critical-path argument is required. Do not multiply this ratio by a
worker-count speedup or an LLVM optimization percentage without a joint model.

QB05. The architecture has a positive runtime-work region, rather than a
universal positive claim. Long-lived movable cohorts with repeated full copying
increase the avoidable term. High survival from young allocation increases
promotion/mature pressure; frequent mature mutation and root replacement
increase barrier/log work. Captured-control roots may dominate every minor.
If weighted_survival=1, this theorem saves zero copying and positive new overhead
can make the candidate worse. Different collection counts, remaining heap
classes and allocation footprints must be charged in the full comparison.

QB06. Do not claim a 50% heap reduction from replacing semispaces. Current
sections also accommodate stack/frame organization, and target young reserves,+mature mark/page metadata, pinned/large objects, fragmentation and worker scratch
remain [Q-S03]. No memory-usage ratio follows from the payload-copy theorem.
Similarly, a concurrent major does not prove a pause bound; the closing cut and
foreign response can dominate. These are separate dimensions in the report.

*** External Quantitative Evidence, Kept in Its Own Domain
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 960ffe6c-d026-5720-92b9-13e7fd737038
:CUSTOM_ID: external-quantitative-evidence-kept-in-its-own-domain
:END:

QB07. The [[https://kcsrk.info/papers/retro-parallel_icfp_20.pdf][OCaml parallelization paper]],
section 6.1, reports that its ConcMinor and ParMinor variants had geometric-mean
sequential running times 4.9% and 3.5% above Stock in that experiment. Its baseline
was OCaml 4.06.1, on a two-socket x86-64 Xeon machine with an isolated-core setup;
these are not present-day OCaml, Darwin or Gambit estimates. The paper's
game_of_life case was about 20% slower, while a large apparent pidigits gain was
sensitive to the baseline compaction policy. This is evidence that policy and
single-thread regressions matter, not a number to transfer to Gambit [Q-P01].
The current [[https://ocaml.org/manual/5.3/parallelism.html][OCaml manual]], section 3,
documents local minor arenas, parallel STW evacuation and concurrent major
collection [Q-P02]. A modern design must expose its actual tradeoffs; generation
count or concurrency alone cannot be its success metric.

*** What Part Is Specifically a Zig Benefit?
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: e5b16ad1-2ae7-507c-a4cf-3c29798796af
:CUSTOM_ID: what-part-is-specifically-a-zig-benefit
:END:

QB08. At the same admitted collector semantics and aligned operation history,
modern C and Zig both obtain the payload-copy reduction in QB02. The
**Zig-exclusive increment in that algorithmic byte-count saving is exactly zero
by the comparison's construction**. Their generated machine cost may differ;
that difference is not established by the byte theorem. This does not make C
modernization free: both full realizations must be funded.

LLVM integration also does not require Zig as a logical intermediary.
[[https://www.llvm.org/docs/GarbageCollection.html][LLVM's GC interface]] supplies
compiler integration hooks while leaving the collector, allocation and root/type
registration to the runtime [Q-P03]. Gambit already has a target-machine
interface [Q-S04]. Completing that interface's shared semantic contract enables
C, Zig and direct LLVM/MLIR providers to compete. Their adapter costs differ;
the same compiler/runtime benefits must not be credited exclusively to Zig.

QB09. At equivalent admitted capability and outcomes, measure implementation and
maintenance in one consistent unit, such as person-months under the same staffing
assumptions. Let:

#+BEGIN_SRC text :eval never
I = Zig upfront realization + migration + verification + transition cost
    minus the corresponding fully funded modern-C costs
S = annual support work avoided by Zig after fair C generator/library reuse,
    excluding the tool/ABI/target/island category charged separately as U
U = annual additional Zig tool/ABI/target/native-island maintenance
T = decision horizon in years
Zig_increment = T*(S-U) - I
positive_Zig_condition: S > U + I/T
#+END_SRC

I may be negative if complete Zig realization costs less initially. Common work
cancels only if truly equal; compare full alternatives from today's checkout.
Use discounting or priced delivery delays when decision-relevant. For interval
estimates, a sufficient conservative condition is
T*(lower(S)-upper(U))-upper(I)>0, with an independently positive total project net.
No project-specific I, S or U interval has yet been established by source reading.

QB10. A useful result is a budget threshold even before an estimate is known:

| Assumed extra Zig upfront I | Assumed annual extra support U | Horizon T | Required annual avoided support S |
|-
| 0 person-months | 1 person-month/year | 3 years | greater than 1 person-month/year |
| 6 person-months | 1 person-month/year | 3 years | greater than 3 person-months/year |
| 6 person-months | 1 person-month/year | 5 years | greater than 2.2 person-months/year |
| 12 person-months | 2 person-months/year | 3 years | greater than 6 person-months/year |
| 12 person-months | 2 person-months/year | 5 years | greater than 4.4 person-months/year |

All I/U/T values here are **decision scenarios, not staffing estimates or a
claim that a GC/runtime rewrite takes six months**. They quantify what Zig must
save under each budget, rather than inventing a saving from language features.
For example, the six/one/three scenario needs more than nine avoided person-months
over the horizon. Equality is break-even, not a positive net.

QB11. Three necessary users do not mean three implementations disappear:

| Necessary service | Support potentially shared in C or Zig | Domain work that remains |
|-
| RootRegistry | Native allocation, typed products, local cleanup | Stable cells, owner/epoch scans, unlink-reader lifetime, transfer receipts |
| BarrierLog | Native buffers, checked sizing, ordinary error paths | Bounded pre-store reservation, no-safepoint commit, ordering and phase drain |
| ReleaseCoordinator | Native task storage and local cleanup | Owner affinity, cancellation, accepted/derived-task accounting and shutdown |

The old services have actual root/store/release users [Q-S01/S03 and the prior
GC ledger]. But a stable root table, a reserved log and a release queue have
different lifetime/backpressure rules. One generic container cannot retire their
algorithms. C-G can share a Scheme generator and C libraries; Zig can share types,
allocator interfaces and error cleanup. Neither the number of clients, source
lines, nor language constructs supplies S. Use scoped maintenance changes and
the actual residual support design to bound it. Exactly zero old paths have
been retired by this research-only study; this is not an estimate of future ROI.

QB12. The present decision is therefore specific: source supports conditional
large reductions in repeated copying, but no unconditional application speedup,
memory ratio, finite pause or full Zig economic lower bound. Advance the
[[file:gambit-modernization-direction.org][language-independent modernization direction]]
and compare full C-G, C-ZT and direct Zig costs. A nonpositive Zig increment
rejects Zig for that scope; it does not reject Gambit's modernization. If the
collector's full workload net is nonpositive, retain/repair the current collector
for that domain while pursuing the compiler/root/FFI separation on its own merits.
