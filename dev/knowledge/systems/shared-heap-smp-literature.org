** A Verified Parallel Scheduler for OCaml 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: parabs
:PUBLICATION_STATUS: PLDI2026; published paper
:READ_SCOPE: abstract; worker blocking/awaiting; limitations and future work
:PRIMARY: https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2026-pldi-parabs.pdf
:END:

[[https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2026-pldi-parabs.pdf][Allain and Scherer]] verify a work-stealing scheduler and a Chase-Lev deque.
The inspected blocking discussion requires stopped workers to block their deque
so another worker cannot wait indefinitely for their response. Crucially, the
proof uses sequential consistency; relaxed-memory reasoning remains future
work. The verified language subset also limits exceptions and effects.

Gambit inference: require an explicit distinction between absent work, an
unanswered observation and a closed participant. Compare this against the
existing take/idle/last-user contract. Do not transplant its deque, assume C23
ordering is proved, or replace multi-shot control with its waiting strategy.

** Reconsidering Garbage Collection in Julia: A Practitioner Report
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: julia-refactoring
:PUBLICATION_STATUS: ISMM2025; published practitioner report
:READ_SCOPE: abstract; sections2.2.3-2.2.4 and3.4
:PRIMARY: https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/julia-ismm-2025.pdf
:END:

[[https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/julia-ismm-2025.pdf][Amorim et al.]] refactor a runtime toward a collector interface. They distinguish
finding live referents from finding every reference requiring repair. Dominated
root omissions and raw foreign pointers complicate movement; pinning has costs.

Gambit inference: use the distinction to audit FrameSpec, captured/active frame
aliases, denv, RC cells and native loans. Reachability alone cannot certify a
moving root adapter. Study the interface boundary, not mandatory MMTk/Rust
adoption; Gambit already moves objects and has different native/control rules.

** Verification of a Generational Garbage Collector
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: verified-generational-c
:PUBLICATION_STATUS: 2026 arXiv preprint; peer-reviewed status not established here
:READ_SCOPE: introduction; mutator interface; future concurrency discussion
:PRIMARY: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13186
:END:

[[https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13186][This Rocq/VST work]] verifies a C multi-generation copying collector with mutable
references and client programs. Its core collector is single-threaded. The
mutator interface exposes allocation space, required capacity and root frames;
parallel collector verification is discussed as further work.

Gambit inference: examine the interface between generated stores, materialized
root slots, allocation bounds and post-GC reload. Its C proof and representation
are not our C23 memory-order proof, FrameSpec adapter or parallel termination
proof. Reuse existing Lean data owners and QNT protocol owners; no proof port is
authorized merely by this paper.

** Iso: Request-Private Garbage Collection
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: iso
:PUBLICATION_STATUS: PLDI2025; published paper
:READ_SCOPE: abstract; isolation background; publication/JNI/array-copy barriers
:PRIMARY: https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/iso-pldi-2025.pdf
:END:

[[https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/iso-pldi-2025.pdf][Qiu and Blackburn]] exploit request-private objects while handling publication

bulk-copy interfaces and native stores. Request boundaries alone do not make
external references disappear.

Gambit inference: privacy would need an explicit escape/publication contract for

policy or transparent actor migration follows. Consider this only after the
actual writer/root boundary is complete.

** Arborescent Garbage Collection: A Dynamic Graph Approach to Immediate Cycle Collection
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: arborescent
:PUBLICATION_STATUS: ISMM2025; published paper
:READ_SCOPE: local publisher PDF, Sections 1, 5, 6 and 7
:PRIMARY: https://doi.org/10.1145/3735950.3735953
:PDF_SHA256: 219c7536f7ecdaec3ceb48a5035067ea532f73b6155d12db7a85889f9cf8e879
:END:

The supplied PDF is the 2025 Arborescent GC paper by Lahaie-Bertrand,
Oest O'Leary, Melançon, Feeley and Monnier. Even and Shiloach is the
dynamic-graph antecedent. The collector maintains a spanning forest and
reverse references, repairing reachability on deletion with a weaker notion
of rank. Section 5 evaluates a Ribbit implementation against mark-and-sweep on 34
of 57 R7RS benchmarks at matched heap sizes per benchmark. Ribs use 11 words
versus 3 in its baseline, with a median slowdown of about 4.5x and a worst
reported slowdown of 8.6x. These figures characterize that prototype only.
Section 6.1 explicitly assumes single-threaded execution and leaves the cost
of multithreading open. Section 6.3 leaves finalizer semantics open.

#+NAME: paper-advantage-admission
| Mechanism | Gambit leverage | Added work / falsifier | Admission |
|-----------+-----------------+------------------------+-----------|
| Feeley safe points and Gambit precise frame information | Publish roots at existing control cuts and give independent workers complete root tasks; preserve multi-shot aliases | Rendezvous tail, root publication/reload and worker scheduling; falsified by missed aliases or stalls under W<R | Main architecture, source and native gates open |
| Parallel block work and generational locality | Split collector work from execution contexts; avoid repeated mature traversal while retaining shared identity | Chunk publication/termination, old-to-young barriers, promotion and space reserve; measure survival, copied bytes and pauses | Main comparison, requires real writer coverage and matched costs |
| Arborescent immediate cycle recovery | Investigate bounded, fully mediated ownership graphs where earlier cycle reclamation has an observable payoff | Reverse-edge storage, update/deletion latency, high fan-in and synchronization; an opaque FFI edge or unbounded mutation path invalidates admission | Separate research candidate; no shared-heap replacement or destructor authority |
| Exact native root handoff | ACK can retire the original owner before the next collection while retaining multi-shot roots | Query pin, write closure/drain and overlapping root storage; reject untracked target membership or old writers | Correctness prerequisite, not an algorithm speedup |

A fair comparison holds the Scheme/FFI semantics fixed. For the immediate
collector candidate measure edge updates, maximum repaired subgraph, mutation
tail latency, retained bytes and metadata peak. For tracing measure barrier
cost, root/remembered scans, copied bytes, pause and peak. Earlier detection
of unreachable nodes does not authorize synchronous foreign destructors;
the owner and effect-delivery contracts still apply.

* RESEARCH Shared semantics are not one physical heap layout
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sharing-versus-heap-organization
:DESIGN_STATUS: distinguish commitments from design variables
:END:

For the existing Gambit thread/runtime contract, preserve admitted object
sharing: reference identity, aliases, observable mutable state, shared closures
and supported multi-shot continuation behavior. This is a migration requirement,
not a claim that the Scheme language requires one physical heap, one GC domain
or this particular processor representation.

#+NAME: sharing-design-freedom
| Preserve / decide explicitly | Not implied by that commitment | Alternative requiring a real protocol |
|------------------------------+--------------------------------+---------------------------------------|
| Same shared object remains observably the same object | All allocation must use one allocator or contiguous region | Context-local allocation, segmented spaces and explicit cross-region edges |
| Mutations through aliases remain observable under the defined concurrency contract | Carrier identity owns roots or heap lifetime | Execution-context roots and separate source/write/collection admission |
| Closures may share captured mutable cells | Every closure/environment is globally shared from birth | Proven private allocation with controlled escape/publication and promotion |
| Multi-shot continuations preserve control and alias semantics | A continuation is an arbitrary transferable actor message or tied forever to one OS thread | Explicit VM/control/resource admission; captured roots and native effects constrain movement |
| Native references remain valid under their admitted contract | Objects never move, or every native borrower permits immediate relocation | Canonical handles/reload, qualified pinning or refusal for unsupported raw pointers |
| Shared objects remain safe during collection | All VMs must rendezvous, every phase uses all carriers, or W must equal R | Defined collection domains, complete root/remembered edges and independent workers |

An explicit isolated actor/VM mode can coexist with shared Scheme execution.
It must define copy, move, immutable sharing or external handles at its boundary;
it cannot silently deep-copy ordinary shared references and claim unchanged
identity/mutation behavior. Similarly, local nurseries do not establish private
object ownership merely by their physical location.

The [[https://kcsrk.info/papers/retro-parallel_icfp_20.pdf][OCaml2020 paper]] is useful here because it examines collector choices against
the language/native interface rather than identifying allocation locality with
isolation. For Gambit, a coordinated minor cut is an existing candidate, not an
axiom. Removing it requires complete cross-region mutation, root, native-access
and memory-order mechanisms with measured costs. Shared-memory semantics alone
neither force the current stop protocol nor prove that it can be removed freely.

Therefore ask which objects actually need sharing, which edges cross collection
domains, who can update them, and when references can be rewritten. Do not start
by declaring either one universal heap or mandatory per-actor isolation. No
architecture decision or native acceptance is issued by this distinction.

*** Mature scan exclusion

Condition the existing =youngReachabilityCovered= and
=rememberedSlotsSelected= laws on the real frozen source view. Every young
strong reachability path either starts in a direct root or has a last
mature-to-young crossing before its young suffix. Enumerate direct roots,
select each actual crossing slot from the frozen remembered set, and trace
the young suffix. Updating all selected source slots plus young interiors
then preserves forwarding even if two different slots point to the same
young object. Mature frame/denv interiors may be skipped only if all their
young outgoing slots are represented, including stores during promotion and
remaining young edges after the previous round. Aliased access to one
physical slot must resolve to that same canonical slot. A moved mature
object would also require its incoming aliases to be rewritten, so the
minor fast path keeps mature objects stationary. Direct roots, weak
processing, RC loans and ordered winding remain in scope.

A frame setter, denv write or FFI borrower absent from the barrier closure
invalidates this argument. A discovered young object with an unrewritten
mature slot is still a failure. On incomplete or overflowed remembered data,
scan the affected mature domain conservatively if its writers are closed;
otherwise refuse the moving cut. A fallback must not silently admit stale
native pointers. The second resume must exercise both a clean skipped
interior and the dirty-slot/overflow negative controls as separate histories.

*** Census elimination and physical peak

Pre-copy admission needs an upper bound even if the mature scan is safely
skipped. From frozen nursery allocation extent and layout, compute demand
for evacuation/promotion of every potentially live nursery allocation plus
stack-to-heap frame expansion not represented by those allocations.
Count aligned sizes, headers and maximum single request. Count nursery
slots in exactly one demand class and charge unbounded frame conversion to
the existing census path. Prove placement for the supported request order
into actual disjoint worker intervals, including fragmentation and rollover.
Reserve the context recovery intervals simultaneously, or prove a worker
tail is transferable at the required cut. Whole-heap free-word totals alone
do not certify this. A failed or unknown reservation leaves source roots
authoritative and no forwarding started; use the admitted census or refuse.
Elimination is a candidate solely for rounds with this independent certificate.

One sufficient packing certificate for an append-only allocator uses separate
worker bins of usable payload B and no straddling. Classify each request by
its charged aligned span, including worst-case local padding. For a worker
with small-request upper bound S and largest charged small request m <= B,
every abandoned small bin consumed at least B-m+1 words when sizes are
integral and rollover occurs only on no-fit. For S > 0, reserving
1 + floor((S-1)/(B-m+1)) small bins therefore covers any order of those
requests. Large requests need their own verified contiguous intervals.
The bound assumes separately budgeted request routes and no other reason
to abandon bins. When winner routing can skew, certify each worker's bound
or enforce a bounded routing policy before forwarding. An aggregate budget
divided by W supplies no such bound. The certificate is sufficient and can
overreserve. Compare that slack against the actual current planner before
claiming a physical-space benefit.

***** Publication and failure rules

Each generated or native store obtains an epoch write permit. Reserve
log capacity before the first pointer mutation; if it cannot be reserved,
publish a sticky dirty-region fallback while the permit is held. A
successful store and its canonical slot/range record become visible
before release of the permit. A bulk producer stages live values in
registered roots and uses overlap-safe copy; after partial failure the
dirty range covers every possibly modified destination until settlement.
An unpublished allocation may bypass per-field logging only under
exclusive initialization, followed by publication of a fully initialized
scan view or its dirty range.

The closer shuts admission atomically and waits for acknowledged writers
and borrowers, with release/acquire handoff for each producer's completed
writes and records. Each worker acquires the sealed snapshot before
claiming a task. Worker writes and next-bank residual records become
visible before task completion; the coordinator acquires completion and
publishes the new bank before restoring contexts. The epoch tag belongs
to the roster, bank and grant, and stale tokens never authorize a store.
The gate does not make an otherwise racing Scheme access safe; existing
object synchronization still applies. The barrier itself must neither
allocate nor call GC, Scheme, or an arbitrary foreign function.

If coverage overflows under a known closed roster, rescan the entire
affected source domain using its precise layout. If physical admission
fails before copying, refuse without changing parity. Lost results retain
the original permit, range or physical grant until settlement proves
the last writer stopped; retrying a raw write or returning a grant on
timeout is unsafe. A stuck participant outside the candidate's reach
does not own its regions. A participant with unknown reach blocks the
affected moving collection.

****** Final observation and commit

cleanup workers have finished. Observe logical table identity, layout
revision, pair indices and both tagged sides, COUNT, relevant FLAGS
and cleanup owner. An oracle must compare exact slot and deletion
outcomes, rather than relying on a lookup that can linear-scan past
KEY_MOVED. Acquire completion of the cleanup/free collective before
sampling. Keep tables and observation storage valid through the cut;
do not dereference old from-space addresses or swept still objects to
confirm a deletion. Test-only events carry pre-captured identity and
post-cleanup fields. For the native index, check lookup and later
writer-owned rehash separately from the pre-reopen slot oracle.

For every final mature-to-next-young edge, publish strong slots as
strong and weak entries as typed weak cleanup work. Final reclassification
supersedes pre-cleanup hints, including deletions and a forwarded survivor
that was promoted out of the next young set. Overflow triggers a
layout-aware range scan with the same strong/weak split. If table
cleanup or a supported foreign effect can still write, no final bank
may be signed. Internal ~move_continuation~ is earlier than this seal;
no context may execute Scheme or regain raw access until it acquires
the epoch-matched resume decision.

Retain a queryable commit identity keyed by VM lifetime, source epoch
and target bank revision. Release-publish the immutable bank and its
commit record at one authoritative transition after worker completion.
On a lost return, acquire-query the exact identity. A committed result
permits the same owner reentry once without republishing or swapping
again. A proven pre-commit refusal permits settlement while retaining
the old bank. An unknown result keeps both banks, typed source custody
and the reentry gate closed. Retire the old bank only after the last
reader has acknowledged or drained and the successor has acquired its
publication; a lost acknowledgement cannot authorize reuse.

****** Invalidation and lifetime protocol

Use distinct identities for the VM incarnation, immutable bank revision,
backing table incarnation and layout revision. A bank pin protects
the bank's metadata lifetime; a separate layout lease protects the
meaning of an indexed slot. A reader of a remembered table record
must acquire the matching layout lease before resolving its offset.
An in-place permutation closes layout admission, drains admitted
layout readers, reserves typed dirty coverage for the whole table
and invalidates the old revision before the first permutation store.
Publish the completed new layout and dirty coverage before reopening
layout readers. Bank-only readers may remain pinned and observe old
metadata, but cannot dereference that old offset in the new layout.
A known revision mismatch is a routing decision to the replacement
coverage, never a reason to drop all work. Revision exhaustion must
refuse reuse until all old identities are retired.

For backing-table replacement, the wrapper's slot update and the
destination table's typed coverage are separate obligations. An old
record cannot redirect its offsets to the replacement. If the old
table remains live, retain its own coverage and lease; if it becomes
unreachable, retire its records only with the appropriate liveness
and last-use evidence. During early Scheme publication preserve
the old source and partial destination as separate rooted layouts.
An exception or unknown result retains both and the dirty obligation
until a completion or safe full route can account for them. A blocked
reader cannot depend on owner reentry to release a layout pin needed
by that same owner; park/release raw access or defer the permutation
before any partial mutation.

At the next freeze, acquire the new bank publication plus the sealed
current table/dirtiness roster. Each claimed table work item resolves
the exact backing identity and revision under lease. For invalidated
records require a covering current typed scan or a proven dead table;
deduplicate per current layout without discarding weak roles. An old
bank ACK is keyed by VM incarnation, bank revision and reader identity.
A lost reply is settled by querying the durable reader-release state.
A known release permits retirement only after all other readers and
layout uses drain. An unknown release retains the bank and any still
borrowed layout; it cannot be converted into a zero count by timeout.
Bank-only retention need not stop the next GC if the successor has
independent coverage and storage within the same physical budget.
Unknown writable layout access blocks moving collection over its
reachable regions. Exhausted retention capacity refuses admission.

****** Stop and takeover

A stop request only closes new admission. A transferable stop receipt
requires the exact executor generation, a release-published final
checkpoint, closed writer/publisher permits and no outstanding helper,
raw access, FIFO gate, private Tail, or child effect outside the
recorded custody. Coordinator acquires this receipt before rotating
the executor generation. A cooperative stop parks after a bounded
effect and can satisfy these conditions. An observed native exit or
successful join can establish that one execution ended, subject to
actual backend and identity, but cannot reconstruct the frontier or
close helpers. =os_thread.c= ~___thread_join~ reports POSIX errors
separately and has a distinct Windows wait path. Unknown join or an
unexpected exit within a nonrecoverable fragment grants no stop receipt.

On confirmed stop, atomically hand the same obligation and settled
frontier to the replacement executor; pin the same sealed layout and
invalidate old writer tokens. If the destination refuses, the VM
keeps the stopped task and its pins. If the transfer reply is lost,
query the transfer identity before issuing another grant; neither
executor may assume it owns the writable frontier.
Keep pending/claimed accounting continuous
through takeover. No idle decrement or task completion until the
remaining scan and all child/weak effects close. Existing all-R
collectives and W termination accounting need an explicit participant
handoff if the carrier exits; a replacement task worker alone cannot
arrive on behalf of an absent root owner. A native run may prove a
cooperative handoff while keeping its carrier parked and counted.
Unknown stop, effect or result keeps the old worker record, bank,
layout, staged effects and root custody. The affected round cannot
retire the bank or reopen owners. Full GC is an eligible pre-copy
route only with independent closure and grants, never a restart after
unsettled forwarding.

***** MVP: atomic stop, frontier transfer and participant custody
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: minor-atomic-stop-mvp
:END:

One DEV MVP covers the full path from stopping a typed scan through
either a successful next-round bank handoff or a justified retained
outcome. It reuses the existing temporal and data proof owners.
Its success criteria here are a source-bound contract, an integrated
native acceptance recipe and a checked SDK projection. Native
implementation and admission remain separate.

The current collector initializes separate strong/weak W counters in
=heap-sections.inc=, then decrements each under the termination mutex
in =collection-round.inc=. A zero counter is phase-local. The same
round's =BARRIER()= resolves to the processor rendezvous in =setup.h=
on SMP builds, and =___root_round_end= checks restored owners across
R. =setup.c= ~setup_sync_op_pstate~ initializes retained synchronization
state after old-set rendezvous and receiver join. This is not an
in-round mechanism for replacing a missing carrier. =object-scanning.inc=
links a weak table to =reached_gc_hash_tables= directly, with no
queryable publication receipt. A stopped worker's private list and
a committed VM weak-task record are therefore distinct states.

****** One atomic authorization handoff

Use a fixed, VM-owned transfer record reserved before forwarding.
Identity binds VM incarnation, collection epoch, phase, task, source
layout revision, and executor generation. The record holds the exact
next operation, settled slot/effect frontier, weak-child identities,
and any retained grants, pins and unresolved obligations. A single
gate or equivalent atomic descriptor transition publishes authorization
and its immutable frontier together. Do not publish a new owner in
one word and reconstruct its frontier later.

| Transition | Required ordering and custody |
|------------+-------------------------------|
| Close old generation | Atomically exclude new worker/helper write and publication permits. Already admitted helpers finish their last actual store or publish, then release their permits. The closer acquires those releases and observes zero before sealing. A check at helper entry or return is insufficient if a store can race between the check and the seal. |
| Seal STOPPED | Verify exact frontier and every in-flight effect under the closed gate, including settled slot repairs, child obligations and no unrecorded FIFO/arena debt. Release-publish one immutable stop descriptor. The VM owns the stopped record, leases and work debt until the transfer commits. An observed carrier death without this settled state grants no seal. |
| Settle weak publication | Install stable child identity and reserve capacity before effect. Publish child ownership and committed query result in one gate transaction. If the return is lost after commit, acquire-query returns the original child receipt, with no reappend or count increment. If not committed, pending intent retains the debt. A non-atomic physical field write needs a bounded, independently
reconcilable effect boundary. A recorded intent alone cannot tell
whether that write or its following publication committed. When
actual effects cannot be reconciled, retain the record and reject
handoff. Do not park waiting for a helper whose completion requires
owner reentry; close only at a cut where existing permits can finish
without that reentry, or quarantine the affected round.

****** W and R responsibility in the same MVP

For a cooperative stop, the original carrier remains parked and
counted in R, with frozen roots and its eventual restore obligation.
Transfer only the logical W work token under the phase's authority.
The transfer preserves exactly one W accounting obligation and
neither increments the original phase membership nor contributes a
false idle decrement. If the destination already executes another
work token, grant it only at an admitted scheduling cut and avoid
blocking its own progress on a W counter it must drain. An MVP can
use a reserved eligible executor or demonstrate a nonblocking
handoff within the existing W budget; charge that reserve explicitly.

For actual carrier exit, its W task and R rendezvous arrival are
separate. Successful task transfer cannot satisfy the missing
processor rendezvous. A future recoverable R exit needs explicit
epoch-bound arrival substitution or a safe membership transition,
including frozen source custody, restore destination, old sync
senders and receiver drain. Current resize/join handling does not
supply this during GC. Keep the original R obligation and frozen
roots on unknown or unsupported exit, block affected-round reopen,
and test retention/quarantine as a negative outcome. Never silently
decrement R, forge a barrier arrival or start a replacement GC after
unsettled forwarding.

****** Close admission and drain two independent exec readers
Two admitted readers retain the same bank across the owner SIGKILL.
The reader pins the backing before publishing its registration under
the admission lock. A fresh recovery process closes admission in the
journal under that lock. A late exec reader observes closure before
opening the backing. A competing recovery process refuses while the
elected writer holds its recovery lock. Close-on-exec prevents that
lock from being inherited by either contender.

The first reader closes its mapping and bank descriptor while the
second still holds a lease. The recovery writer independently checks
that the exclusive bank lease is still unavailable. The last reader
syncs its ACK and exits. Exclusive lease acquisition and replay of
both ACK identities allow one unlink and a terminal record. A further
restart sees the same terminal journal and absent bank. The existing
post-census planner refusal and full GC retry run with unchanged map
and PBT inputs at R=3/W=2. The first suite timed out in an earlier
single-reader control after a GC pass marker. The exact stalled endpoint
is unresolved. The retained final suite exited zero under the same
30-second watchdog with the same collector and map executable.
