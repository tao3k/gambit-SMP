## Earlier source substrate and proposed overlap design

`include/gambit.h.in:11801..11808` embeds sync_id0/1/2, sync_op0/1/2 and sync_mut/cv in each pstate. `setup.c:705..732` acquires flags, release-publishes flags and then unlocks/signals. In barrier_sync_op, a child flag is reset at790 before the copied operation is read at792; the complete old tree protocol supplies sequencing not expressible by that flag alone. This report does not assert that this existing ordering is independently a demonstrated defect. A new overlapping epoch protocol cannot inherit it without proving its new reuse discipline.

Adopt independent, pinned epoch/round channels and explicit storage admission. Do not reuse a pstate mailbox, decrement an old pin before the signal returns, or acquire a refcount through an unprotected stale pointer. Add a stable retired-record ledger reference; keep it through destructor failure, rather than trying to resurrect an object after its refcount became zero. Separate ordinary RC ownership from native metadata/queue/scanner pin ownership.

## Proposed state, fields and C11 ordering

Use native descriptor fields `{full_key, rank_map, frozen_domains, channels, gate, retire_state}`; gate is one atomic mode/count word. Its count includes one stable registry/retired-ledger reference plus all admitted operation, wait, native-tail and queued-reader references. Per-channel key is `{vm_incarnation,cohort_epoch,descriptor_nonce,round,phase,sender_incarnation}`. A carrier/reclaim record additionally includes `{native_owner_incarnation,job_nonce,restore_generation,carrier_generation}`. A physical slot/address or parity bit is not an identity.

Suggested modes: OPEN → CLOSING → FINALIZING → FREED; a failed known-safe destruction step goes from FINALIZING to QUARANTINED while preserving ledger ownership. Pin count overflow, epoch exhaustion or unsupported key mismatch refuses before acceptance; never silently wrap to a live historical key. Retired descriptor capacity is bounded; a full pool backpressures a new transition while the current stable cohort continues service. Do not allocate unbounded replacement descriptors to disguise a permanently pinned predecessor.

| Edge | Required ordering and lifetime argument |
| --- | --- |
| Initial lookup → pin | Caller first holds a valid VM entry/borrow. Under the stable VM cohort-control lock, resolve pointer and full key, then successful acquire/acq_rel gate update adds the ref before unlocking. Publisher/retirer uses the same lock to remove discoverability. A raw atomic load of pointer followed by fetch_add on it is unsafe: the object can die in between. |
| Close → successor publication | Under that control authority, CAS OPEN→CLOSING with acq_rel while preserving count; initialize successor ranks/domains/channels fully before release-publishing it. Getters acquire-observe and revalidate full identity. Existing counted consumers stay legal; new external discovery is refused. Do not hold this lock across a collective wait or P/T-lock acquisition. |
| Payload → FULL | Ordinary payload/key stores are sequenced before FULL release; receiver's matching FULL acquire makes them visible. Notification can coalesce/fail without erasing the persisted FULL/pending obligation. Raw SCMOBJ payload needs a registered root, not merely this ordering. |
| Last payload read → EMPTY → overwrite | Receiver finishes/copies every payload field before EMPTY release. Next sender's EMPTY acquire orders its overwrite after those reads. Full round/phase tags prevent accepting another round's FULL; native pin prevents accessing freed storage. Tags alone do not provide the latter. |
| Native tail end → pin put | Unlock, condition wait/signal return, payload forwarding and all descriptor/pstate/backend accesses finish before an acq_rel decrement. Use acq_rel for the initial implementation rather than an optimized relaxed/release-only variant. Each live dependent backend/pstate is itself owned/pinned by the descriptor/VM ledger; pinning only a small header does not protect an independently freed sync_cv. |
| Closed ledger-only → destruction | An acq_rel claim observes prior consumer drops and takes FINALIZING while keeping the ledger ref. Only after all native resources are known destroyed does the owner consume the ledger ref and free storage. No new borrower can race this transition because discovery is closed and no nonledger ref exists to authorize an internal clone. |
| Queue handoff → consumer last release | Acquire/move/clone a held capability **before** release-publishing an event; consumer acquire-observes it and holds through its last access. A wake hint or an event containing an unpinned pointer is insufficient. Posting an event after dropping the last reference is forbidden. |

These edges are the required conditional happens-before derivation, not evidence that new C atomics are implemented or a C11 model checker proved them. Shared-lock unknown results require the reviewed shared-fault/terminal policy; a local gate/refcount cannot restore an unknown mutex ownership state.

Consumer `put` is its final ordinary descriptor access. The exclusively claimed owner may inspect/destroy the record before actual free; that is distinct from a borrower using a pointer after final release. Owner-affine RC cleanup is queued while ledger ownership still exists. Do not enqueue a raw pointer after decrementing it to zero, and do not call ___addref_rc on a record whose list membership or lifetime has already ended.

## ABA and two handoffs

The combined model makes E0→E1→E2, with physical mailbox slot0 reused by E2. While E0 native tail or its queued reader remains pinned, slot0 cannot be destroyed/reused. E1 uses an independent slot and can perform GC/reclaim an unrelated carrier. After both E0 consumers end, its ledger can destroy it and initialize slot0 for generation2. Historical token0 is then rejected by full generation lookup before dereferencing any retired pointer. Token0 and2 have equal parity: the modulo-based negative accepts a stale capability and violates ownership.

A late E0 sender writes E0's own channel while pinned. The Alias negative instead directs that concrete write to E1's channel: m1 changes from1 to0 and successor-key integrity fails. This is stronger than the predecessor model's structural 'channels must be distinct' assertion, but it still models an abstract keyed write, not an observed native C corruption trace.

Full keys must be checked in a stable registry **before** resolving an untrusted historical pointer. For callbacks returning during VM close, an already-admitted internal capability remains valid under its counted lane; a new public request with an old VM/owner token is rejected. Copying a ticket without a pin or keeping an arbitrary foreign-address escape is outside this support contract. Key widths and overflow refusal must be explicit in the implementation; a bounded E0/E1/E2 proof is not proof against arbitrary integer wrap.

## Two carriers, GC and busy skipping

This slice starts after both carriers have valid destination-root installation ACKs, as specified in [the preceding source handoff](cohort-ack-rc-protocol.md); it does not infer those ACKs from GO or marker death. A retains a native borrow, B initially retains a separate reader. A stable owner window excludes root scanning/mutation races; the bounded batch visits two records. If A is busy, keep its RC root and reclaim debt, advance cursor, then visit B. Once B's reader exits, B is reclaimable even when A's borrow and the E0 tail are permanently retained. GC between batches scans actual still-live RC roots and the installed destination roots.

Normal mode later releases A's borrow; a later batch reclaims A exactly once. BusyA keeps A's borrow forever; OldTailHeld keeps the predecessor tail forever and prevents slot0 reuse, but neither prevents B's eventual reclaim under the model's fair service and B-reader completion assumptions. It is not a promise that A or the old descriptor eventually frees.

A production batch should: capture bounded cutoff and a pinned cursor; nonblocking claim record/full key; close new **native reader** admission after root ACK; check its outstanding borrow/ACK/native-reader units; check GC reader grace in the common owner window; either keep and advance or unlink locally once. Closing native readers does not permit dropping GC coverage while h->data remains linked. Busy/unknown records stay rooted; completed records do not strand a cursor in freed memory. One busy record must not add a global all-records-zero precondition.

The GlobalGate temporal negative adds exactly that global condition: A's permanent borrow disables every batch, so safe roots remain but B never frees. StuckCursor leaves the cursor on busy A until the budget expires, repeating each round; B again starves despite safe memory. Positive progress is conditional on fair bounded windows and eventual release of B's own reader, not on release of unrelated A or the predecessor tail.

## Grok implementation and native validation order

1. Define full keys, finite capacity/overflow refusal, descriptor-owned epoch/round channels and stable VM lookup authority. Audit setup failure/cleanup and every current/successor getter before replacing mailbox macros. A checked lookup may not start by dereferencing a stale pointer.
2. Implement counted tail/queue/wait handles and the CLOSED/FINALIZING/QUARANTINED ledger protocol. Cover pstate/backend resources as well as descriptor bytes. Bind actual accepted internal moves/clones to the same gate and close policy; do not treat ordinary RC count as this gate.
3. Move barrier send/receive to pinned descriptor channels. Publish key/payload before FULL, consume before EMPTY, validate phase, and put pins after actual wait/unlock/signal/forwarding tails. Preserve common ACK/GO and GC debt from the previous handoffs; no local early return or new numeric opcode substitute.
4. Implement bounded owner batch with per-record claims, pinned cutoff/cursor, busy skip and independent RC unlink/native metadata release. Preserve generated completion-root ACK and actual h->data scanning. Do not free a record with a remaining queue reader simply because the native signal returned.
5. On one source/header/generated/object/link-bound producer, pause E0 after FULL publication but before unlock/signal tail end; publish E1 and send E1 traffic/GC. Verify independent channels, live old pins and no successor payload overwrite. Keep a queued E0 reader after native tail exit; E0 must stay live until that reader drops.
6. Reuse the same physical slot for E2 only after E0 grace. Submit historical token0, a modulo-colliding epoch, stale VM/owner incarnation and wrong round/phase. Require rejection before freed-pointer access; live accepted internal capabilities still drain during close. Test count/epoch exhaustion refusal, without widening time budgets.
7. Hold A's native borrow and E0 tail; release B's own reader and run repeated GC/owner batches. B must free once while A stays rooted/pinned. Then release A and require a later bounded batch to reclaim it. Repeat with arrivals after cutoff, duplicate ACK/put attempts and GC between destination ACK/RC release. Observe actual relocated cells and native last-access positions.
8. Inject descriptor allocation/setup/destruction failures and unknown lock results separately. Known failed preparation retains the active cohort; unknown shared state uses the reviewed shared-fault disposition. Preserve ledger/root ownership on uncertain destruction; do not fabricate a successful free or resurrect zero-count memory.

Independent sufficiency judgment: the combined finite model can establish operational mailbox separation, retained consumer lifetime, a bounded ABA collision check and noninterference of A's permanent borrow with B's conditional progress. It cannot establish actual C11 ordering, generated-root correctness, arbitrary epoch/round wrap, concurrent multi-producer mailbox arbitration, collector weak/finalizer semantics, VM shutdown/unknown-commit recovery or same-producer native correctness. Those remain implementation/refinement and targeted native gates, not reasons to count this as9/12.
